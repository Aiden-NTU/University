# The brief and our response

## The brief

The project brief asks each team to design a sustainable space where people live or work, to
address one or more of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), and to integrate
a variety of sustainable technologies [@ntu_brief]. The design may be new or may redesign part
of the Clifton Campus. It must show research into technologies, materials and practices,
creativity, practicality for the people who use it, and an attempt to analyse its costs. Our
outputs are technical drawings, a poster and this Design Outline.

## Our response

We chose a new building on a real site: the Students' Car Park at the northern edge of the
Clifton Campus. A car park is a good place to make the case for sustainable design. It is a
large area of hard, hot, impermeable tarmac that is busy for a few hours a day and empty for
the rest, and it encourages the most carbon-intensive way of getting to campus.

The Heat Exchange replaces most of it with a learning hub that students would use all day: a
place to study, teach, make things, eat and park a bike. The name has two meanings. The
building exchanges heat, because the warmth from its own computers heats its rooms. It is
also a place where students from different courses exchange ideas, which is why the teaching
hall, makerspace and library sit side by side around a shared atrium.

We set ourselves five design aims:

1. **Use energy twice.** Treat waste heat from computing as the building's main heat source
   ([[Heat from computers]]).
2. **Generate on site.** Cover the roof in solar panels without losing daylight
   ([[Solar power]]).
3. **Need less in the first place.** A compact form, a timber structure, shading that follows
   the seasons and efficient lighting ([[Form, daylight and shading]]).
4. **Give the ground back.** Swap tarmac for meadow, a pond and a rain garden, and make cycling
   the easy way to arrive ([[The site]]).
5. **Teach with the building.** Show its live energy data to the students inside it
   ([[Smart building control room]]).

## The goals we chose

The design responds most strongly to Goals 7, 9, 12 and 13, with Goals 4 and 11 as secondary
goals. [[How the design addresses the SDGs]] maps each design feature to the goal and target
it supports.
