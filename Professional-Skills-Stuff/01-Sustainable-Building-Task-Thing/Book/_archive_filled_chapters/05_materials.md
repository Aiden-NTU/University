# Materials and construction

## A timber structure

The walls, floors and roof are built from cross-laminated timber (CLT): large panels made by
gluing layers of timber boards at right angles to each other, which makes them strong in both
directions. Panels are cut to size in a factory and craned into place, so the building goes up
quickly with little waste or noise on site.

Timber was chosen mainly for its carbon. Steel and concrete both release large amounts of CO₂
when they are made, while a tree takes CO₂ out of the air as it grows and keeps it locked in
its wood for as long as the timber lasts. @churkina2020 argue that building cities from
engineered timber instead of concrete and steel could turn buildings into a long-term store of
carbon, provided the timber comes from sustainably managed forests.

## The external wall

The external wall is {{ext_wall_mm}} mm thick. Each layer has a job:

Table: External wall construction, outside to inside

| Layer | Thickness | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Timber cladding on battens | 40 mm | Weather protection; the gap behind lets the wall dry out |
| Wood-fibre insulation | 220 mm | Keeps heat in; also made from timber, and stores carbon |
| Cross-laminated timber | 100 mm | Structure, airtightness and the finished inside face |

Wood-fibre insulation is heavier than most plastic foam insulation, so it also slows down the
flow of heat on hot summer days, which helps stop the rooms from overheating.

## Targets for the whole building

Industry bodies in the UK have set targets for how much energy and embodied carbon new
buildings should use if the country is to meet its climate commitments. The LETI Climate
Emergency Design Guide [@leti2020] and the RIBA 2030 Climate Challenge [@riba2021] both set
targets for operational energy use and for the carbon embodied in materials, and both
recommend measuring a building's performance after it opens rather than relying only on design
predictions. The live monitoring in the control room ([[Smart building control room]]) would
make that possible.

## Fit-out materials

The same thinking carries into the furniture, which is listed item by item in
[[Itemised fit-out schedule]]:

- **Reused before new.** Office desks and task chairs are refurbished or remanufactured, which
  avoids most of the embodied carbon of making new ones.
- **Recycled content.** Stacking chairs have recycled-plastic shells, sofas are upholstered in
  recycled-fibre fabric and the lockers in the changing room are made from recycled steel.
- **Certified and reclaimed timber.** Tables, desks and bookcases are made from FSC-certified
  timber, and the café counter and the high bench tables in the lounge from reclaimed timber.
- **Built to adapt.** Flip-top teaching tables fold and nest, so the teaching hall can change
  from lectures to workshops to events without buying different furniture.
