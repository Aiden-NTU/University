# The site

## Where it is

The site is the Students' Car Park on the northern edge of NTU's Clifton Campus, Nottingham
NG11 8NS. The building's south-west corner is at latitude {{lat}}° N, longitude {{lon}}° {{lon_dir}}
(British National Grid {{easting}} E, {{northing}} N), and its ground floor is set at {{ffl}} m
above sea level. The plot is bounded by woodland and a cricket ground to the north-west, sports
pitches to the south-east and teaching buildings to the south-west.

## What is there now

Today the plot is an open tarmac car park. Hard surfaces like this send rainwater straight into
drains instead of letting it soak into the ground, absorb the sun and stay warm into the evening,
and support almost no wildlife. The car park is also a statement about how people are expected
to reach the campus.

We built our picture of the site from open data rather than guesswork:

- **Ground levels and heights** come from the Environment Agency's 1 m LiDAR surveys. The terrain
  model gives the bare ground [@ea_dtm], and subtracting it from the first-return surface model
  [@ea_dsm] gives the heights of the surrounding trees and buildings.
- **Roads, paths and building outlines** come from OpenStreetMap [@osm].
- **Aerial imagery** comes from Esri's World Imagery service [@esri_imagery].

## What we propose

Most of the tarmac is replaced. The building sits towards the centre of the plot with its long
side facing south. Around it, a wildflower meadow, a rain garden and a pond take the place of
hard surfacing. The rain garden and pond slow down storm water and give it somewhere to soak
away, and the meadow supports insects and birds. New paths connect the building to the existing
campus routes.

Some parking stays, because not everyone can cycle or take the bus. The retained spaces sit to
the east under solar carports, which shade the cars and generate electricity from a surface that
would otherwise do nothing. The cycle hub on the east end of the building has {{bike_spaces}}
secure spaces, e-bike charging, showers and changing rooms, so cycling becomes the easiest way to
arrive.
