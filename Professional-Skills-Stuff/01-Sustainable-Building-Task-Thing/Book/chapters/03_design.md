# Design concept

## Overall concept and organisation

The Heat Exchange is a two-storey timber learning hub organised so that it fits into the Clifton
Campus ecosystem. The concept is deliberately ambitious about transforming the surrounding
landscape, while remaining practical for everyday users [@ntu_brief]. Taking inspiration from
the Clifton Campus library, rooms are arranged by intended noise level: teaching and social
spaces, focused study, and quieter wellbeing uses sit apart so people can choose the environment
they need.

The main block is 24 m by 14.4 m on a 4.0 × 4.8 m structural grid, with a single-storey cycle hub
(6 × 9.6 m) on the east. Public and daytime rooms face south toward campus and daylight; plant,
servers and WCs sit in a north strip. A double-height atrium links the floors.

## Envelope, appearance and users

Externally the building reads as a timber construction with generous glazing and a **sawtooth
roof** carrying solar panels on the south-facing slopes. The north faces of the teeth are glazed
to bring soft daylight deep into the first floor without the glare of direct sun—the same logic
used in many industrial north-light roofs, here combined with PV on the opposite slope.

Table: Envelope summary

| Element | Proposal | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structure | Engineered timber (CLT) primary frame and floors | Stores carbon in the structure [@churkina2020] |
| External wall | about 360 mm overall | Timber / masonry outer leaf with hempcrete thermal wrap — see [[Materials, research and the SDGs]] |
| Roof | Sawtooth; PV on 25° south slopes | North light + generation; 1.2 m south overhang shades summer sun |
| Glazing | Higher on south (about 49% WWR) and west; lower on north (about 20%) | Winter sun welcome on south; north kept for heat loss |

Day-to-day use assumes regular classes, a student-run café open roughly 09:00–17:00 in term time,
and accessible toilets. Inclusive design follows Approved Document M for non-domestic buildings:
level entrance, 1.5 m corridors, a lift, and accessible WCs [@adm2015]. Fire strategy would follow
Approved Document B (means of escape, compartmentation and detection appropriate to a teaching
building) [@adb2019]. External doors would use motorised or “press to open” accessible operation
consistent with NTU estates practice across campus; the exact ironmongery brand would follow the
university’s approved supplier list. Non-gendered accessible WC provision supports Goal 5
(equality) in day-to-day use [@un2015].
