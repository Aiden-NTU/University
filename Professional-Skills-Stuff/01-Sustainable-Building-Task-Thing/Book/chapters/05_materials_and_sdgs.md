# Materials, research and the SDGs

## Materials strategy

Materials research for the team (summarised from the Sustainability Lead’s presentation slides)
compared several low-carbon options and then aligned them with how Clifton already looks.

**Structure and outer leaf.** Engineered **timber** remains the primary frame: it is strong,
relatively light, and can cut embodied carbon substantially compared with steel and concrete
[@churkina2020]. For the visible outer leaf, the team also researched **K-Briq** units—unfired
blocks made from near-100% construction debris, with manufacturer claims of about 30 N/mm²
compressive strength, very low embodied carbon per unit, and an aesthetic close to conventional
brick so the building can sit with existing campus fabric without painted cladding [@kenoteq].

**Walls and insulation.** **Hempcrete** (hemp shiv with a lime binder) is proposed as the thermal
wrap / infill: it combines insulation with moisture buffering, helps keep indoor humidity in a
comfortable band, and is argued to be carbon-negative over its life as the crop grows and the
lime carbonates [@hempcrete]. In combination with a timber frame and K-Briq outer leaf, the wall
build-up targets the model’s overall thickness of about **360 mm**.

**Interior finishes.** Recycled **carpet tiles** allow local replacement without full re-carpeting.
**Demountable glass partitions** keep daylight moving through the plan and reduce waste if layouts
change. **Zero-VOC paints** cut airborne chemicals at fit-out. Together these choices support
healthier indoor air (Goal 3) and responsible materials use (Goal 12) [@un2015].

## Mapping to the Sustainable Development Goals

We address the Goals in three bands, matching the project aims [@un2015]:

Table: Design features mapped to SDG targets

| Band | Goals | Examples in this design | Targets (examples) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Health, education, equality | 3, 4, 5 | Daylight, IAQ-aware ventilation, wellbeing room; teaching / library / makerspace; accessible and non-gendered WCs | 3.9, 4.7, 5.1 |
| Resources and infrastructure | 6, 7, 8, 9 | Rain-soft landscape; roof PV and heat recovery; student jobs in the café; edge computing + smart control room | 6.3, 7.2, 7.3, 9.4 |
| Climate and partnership | 13–17 | Timber and low-carbon materials; landscape replacing hardstanding; open data and shared digital twin; campus / city transport links | 13.3, 15.1, 17.17 |

The strongest technical claims sit with **Goals 7 and 9** (clean energy and innovative
infrastructure) and **Goal 13** (climate action through fabric and landscape). Goals 4 and 5 are
served by the learning programme and inclusive access rather than by plant alone.
