The PDF's within this directory are Tagged PDFs, with the exception of the polar coordinates PDF which fails to tag due to its contents. A tagged PDF is specifically built to contain metadata to help those who are reliant on screen readers, to allow them to access the content with ease.

The documents themselves are sourced directly from NTU's NOW Online Workspace.

The math module end-goal is stated to be :
"
By the end of this module, you should be able to:

    Make sense of graphs displaying complex data
    Analyse lab/experimental results with simple descriptive statistics
    Rearrange equations to isolate variables
    Use calculus to pull information from curves
    Solve simple problems in geometry and probability
    Use vectors to describe combining forces.
"

So I would personally focus on getting a grasp of each of the above to some degree. Pun not intended. OK it was.
As the math module is shared between a variety of courses, it may be the case that some of the math isn't particularly relevant to CS specifically, especially since programming is almost always a process of delegating complexity to underlying standard libraries which you rarely need to know the complexity or inner workings of (Who actually cares how Python's Timsort actually works, we just know it does.)

So for the most part it helps to just know the syntax for a variety of equations, and the general principles.
However if you happen to be studying a non CS course, perhaps physics or some other science, you may find it useful to focus on certain parts more than others, depending on what best fits your overall course content.
